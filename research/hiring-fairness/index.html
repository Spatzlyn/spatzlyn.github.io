What Does a Fairness Audit Establish?: Evidence from Score-Based Selection in AI Hiring Assessments

Yookyung Youn

Korea University

ResultsGitHub

Abstract

Fairness audits of score-based assessments can support different conclusions depending on what is measured and how many candidates are selected. Using cognitive-task scores from the Human Connectome Project as a controlled analogue for hiring assessments, we examine whether reducing demographic information in scores narrows group disparities, whether improvements persist across selection rates, and how often individual selection decisions change after mitigation. The case study shows that a favorable finding at a single cutoff can reverse at another. The selections are simulated; the dataset contains no employer hiring decisions.

One model, two different conclusions

At a 20% selection rate, mild adversarial mitigation moved the gender selection-rate ratio closer to parity, from 0.715 to 0.760. At 25%, the same intervention moved it farther from parity, from 0.803 to 0.785. Each model’s ranking stayed fixed as the cutoff changed.

What else changes?

Gender and age can move in different directions

With stronger mitigation (λ = 5), the lowest gender ratio across the five cutoffs rose from 0.631 to 0.717. The lowest age ratio fell from 0.595 to 0.472 over the same sweep.

A fixed quota can select different people

At the 20% cutoff, 1.14% of participants changed selection status under mild mitigation, compared with 11.02% under stronger mitigation. These counts include people who entered and people who left the selected group.
